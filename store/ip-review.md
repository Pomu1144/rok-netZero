# IP review: distance from Rise of Kingdoms

Game mechanics (city building, marches, generals, alliances) are not protected by
copyright. Names, art, text and distinctive presentation can be. This file records
what was changed to keep Realm of Kings clearly its own game. It is not legal
advice: have a lawyer review it before launch.

## Already distinct

- All art is original and in a sumi-e ink style; no assets, icons, music or text come from Rise of Kingdoms.
- The generals are historical figures (public domain), with original portraits, titles and skills.
- No VIP tiers, no paid gems shop, no pay-to-win.

## Features Rise of Kingdoms lacks

- **Build plan**: queue up to three upgrades that start themselves when a builder frees up and the cost is in your stores, offline included.
- **Best speedups**: one tap spends the exact mix of speedups that finishes a timer with the least waste.
- **Harvest all**: one button on the city screen collects every farm, mill, quarry and mine.
- **Free talent resets**, **battle replays** with skill cut-ins, and alliance help on every queue.

## Open risks for the owner

1. **The name.** "Realm of Kings" abbreviates to "RoK", the same as Rise of Kingdoms, and both are "___ of King(s/doms)". This is the biggest risk. Run a trademark search (USPTO, EUIPO, app stores) and consider a name that doesn't shorten to RoK.
2. **HUD layout.** The city HUD follows the genre's familiar arrangement on request. Layout is rarely protectable, but the ink look should stay the main visual identity.
3. **Store keywords.** Never use "Rise of Kingdoms", "RoK" or "Lilith" in the listing, keywords or ads.
